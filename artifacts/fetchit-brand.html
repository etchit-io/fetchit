The reader's companion. · Built on the same system. Carries a different verb.

fetchit is the consumer companion to etchit — the browser, the reader, the share target. Where etchit etches content into Autonomi, fetchit retrieves it. The two ship as a pair: one verb writes, the other returns. This document inherits the etchit visual system in full; everything you read here is what changes when the verb does. Tokens, type, color, and voice carry over verbatim. The mark, the character, and the surfaces are fetchit's own.

01 / Principles

Five rules, one direction.

fetchit answers to etchit's principles, plus its own. When in doubt, pick the one closest to retrieve and the one furthest from perform.

01
Companion by design.

fetchit is never a standalone brand. It is the second word in a sentence whose first word is etchit. Lockup, copy, and surface treatments should always read like a sibling — never a sequel, never a sub-brand, never a separate product story.

02
Receive, never perform.

fetchit's job is to render exactly what was etched — no recommendations, no remixing, no "for you." If the writer chose monospace, you show monospace. If the writer chose nothing, you show nothing. The reader came for the content, not the chrome.

03
Terminal, not tech.

Inherited from etchit. We speak monospace, not marketing. A hash looks like a hash. An address looks like an address. The interface should feel like something a developer left running — not a thing the marketing team styled.

04
The chevron is the brand.

> is not a stylistic choice, it's the identity. It appears in the wordmark, the mark, the favicon, the prompt, the forward arrow. Treat it like a logomark: never flip it, never color it anything but copper, never replace it with an angle bracket or arrow glyph.

05
Speed reads as care.

fetchit ships one experience metric: time-to-first-character. Every design decision either lowers it or it doesn't. No splash screens, no progressive blur-ups, no skeleton states that pretend. Either the content arrived or it didn't — show whichever is true.

// rules of speech
How fetchit refers to itself in copy.

fetchit is always lowercase, even at the start of a sentence — same as etchit. Brand names are pronouns of last resort: prefer it once introduced. Never write "the fetchit app" or "Fetchit" — both violate the lowercase rule. Inside copy, the chevron treatment (fetch>it) only appears in display contexts; in running prose it's plain fetchit.

05 / Type

Instrument Serif italic appears only as accent — chapter emphasis, pull quotes, the rare editorial flourish. 06 / Voice

Verb first. Always.

fetchit speaks etchit's voice with one tilt: it leads with the action. Short. Imperative. Slightly impatient — as if the content has already arrived and you're just catching up.

Do
Don't
Fetch a doc.
Welcome to the future of permanent reading.
Paste an address. Read.
Simply enter your unique content identifier.
Already there.
Loading your content, please wait...
No login. No tracking. Just the doc.
Your privacy is our top priority.
Bad address.
We're sorry, but the requested resource could not be located.
Etched 2 minutes ago.
This document was recently published by an anonymous user.

Rule of thumb: every microcopy string should fit in one breath. If you'd say it slowly, you're saying too much. Verbs do most of the work (fetch, read, share, copy). Adjectives almost never appear; when they do, they earn it.

// the chevron vs. the slash
Two glyphs, two verbs, one system.

etchit's slash and fetchit's chevron are deliberately the same weight, same color, same family. Their visual difference is one stroke vs. two — the direction of the verb made literal. The slash inscribes, the chevron points. Together they read as the full motion of permanent publishing: mark, retrieve.

08 / Motion

Fetch in. Settle. Never load.

fetchit inherits etchit's mechanical motion philosophy — no springs, no overshoots, no easing that smiles. One addition: the arrival.

Arrival
0.2s · ease-out · opacity 0 → 1
The moment content paints from the network. Single fade-in, no skeleton, no shimmer, no progress bar. Either it arrived or it didn't.
Address focus
0.15s · ease · border-color
Address bar gains a copper border on focus. Cursor blinks step-end (1.1s) — terminals don't fade.
Cursor blink
1.1s · step-end · on/off
Inherited from etchit. A single copper block cursor in the address bar prompt.
Fade-up (scroll)
0.8s · ease-out · translateY(30 → 0)
Inherited. Section reveal triggered by IntersectionObserver. One section at a time; no cascading.
Pulse
2s · infinite · opacity + scale(1 → 1.3)
Inherited. The status dot in the eyebrow. Slow, quiet — signal of life, not attention-grab.
Hash copy
0.4s · ease · color flash
Click-to-copy on an Autonomi address: brief flash to bone, instant return to copper. Confirms the action without a toast.
Reader scroll
native
No custom scroll, no parallax, no momentum tweaks. The reader scrolls like a document because it is one.

Rule of thumb: the goal of every animation is to vanish. If the user notices the motion, the motion failed. Correct verbs: arrives, settles, blinks, paints. Wrong verbs: loads, animates, transitions, builds.
